# ProjectPidia — Development Opportunity Infrastructure

**Status:** Emerging venture / product definition  
**Role represented here:** Product concept development and technical planning  
**Implementation claim:** This case study documents product direction and requirements exploration; it does not claim a completed or publicly deployed platform.

## The problem

Communities often have development priorities but lack a consistent way to document needs, validate proposals, structure project information, and present opportunities to potential funders or development partners.

## Product direction

ProjectPidia is intended to provide a structured pathway for communities to:

1. Identify and submit development priorities.
2. Document needs, locations, evidence, and intended outcomes.
3. Review and validate submissions through defined workflows.
4. Organize priorities into fundable project profiles.
5. Connect suitable opportunities with institutions, funders, development partners, and resource providers.
6. Track progress and maintain accountability as projects move forward.

## Engineering considerations

- Role-based access for community members, reviewers, administrators, and partners.
- Clear submission states and an auditable review history.
- Data validation and duplicate detection for incoming priorities.
- Document and evidence management with controlled access.
- Search and filtering across geography, sector, readiness, and funding status.
- Privacy, consent, and protection of sensitive community information.
- Reporting that distinguishes submitted, validated, funded, and completed projects.

## MVP direction

A sensible first release would focus on authenticated submissions, structured project records, an administrative review queue, status history, and shareable project summaries. Partner matching and advanced analytics can follow after the core data and validation process are proven.

## What this work demonstrates

Product thinking around multi-stakeholder workflows, information quality, governance, and the translation of a broad social-development problem into a staged software platform.
